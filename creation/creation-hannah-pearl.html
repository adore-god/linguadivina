The Court & The Creation

1 Samuel 1 — Hannah and Peninnah — The Favored and the Jewel

And the other wife did everything possible to make her unhappy, because the Lord had not let her have children; And year by year, whenever she went up to the house of the Lord, she kept on attacking her, so that Hannah gave herself up to weeping and would take no food. — 1 Samuel 1:6–7

Hannah has no children. Peninnah has sons and daughters. Year after year the second wife attacks the first at the house of the court, until Hannah weeps and will not eat. This is not a story about rivalry between two women. It is a demonstration of what the court does with two named states set on one ground: it does not remove either of them, and it does not move Hannah until a claim is filed from inside the enclosure. The Genesis creation pattern is in the names before the chapter opens. The court's instrument here is the name itself, two identity codes, Favored and Jewel, enforced after their kind.

Peninnah — Names as Identity Codes

Peninnah (Strong's H6444) is "jewel" in BDB's outline. Strong's says only "probably feminine from H6443 contracted", and H6443 it glosses "probably a pearl (as round)" while the KJV prints it "ruby" and Gesenius reads "coral" or "pearl". The lexicons differ on the stone, so the stable reading is something precious and set on display. Her name is her function, and the text shows it in the first lines: she "was the mother of children" (1:2), and Elkanah gives "to Peninnah his wife, and to all her sons and daughters, their part of the feast" (1:4). After its kind, Genesis 1:11-12 puts seed in the plants and the fruit-trees, and Peninnah is the state that already holds the visible seed.

Hannah — The State Named Favored, Holding No Seed

Hannah (Strong's H2584, from chanan, H2603) is "favored". She occupies the condition that contradicts her name: "Hannah had no children" (1:2), and "the Lord had not let her have children" (1:5). The text puts the holding with YHVH, present consciousness, and Elohim — the judges and rulers — enforce what is held after its kind. The ground itself is not lacking. Her husband's ground is named in 1:1 as Ephraim, "double fruit", and she goes up to Shiloh, "tranquil" in Strong's. The one missing piece is on the woman, and it is seed. The enclosure is not the punishment. It is the state the court requires before it can enforce a new identity, because Elohim cannot deliver what has not been filed.

The Attacking — Genesis 1:28, Two States on One Ground

Genesis already holds this pair. Rachel has no children and Leah has sons (Genesis 29-30), and the verdict there comes to the wife who had none, when the Judges remember Rachel and she conceives (Genesis 30:22). The same mechanism is running here. The blessing of fruitfulness in Genesis 1:28 sits in one house on one wife, and the other wife is shown it every year. Peninnah does "everything possible to make her unhappy" (1:6), and "year by year, whenever she went up to the house of the Lord" she "kept on attacking her" (1:7). The text does not say the attacking forms anything. It says what the attacking does: Hannah weeps and will not eat. Then Elkanah speaks: "am I not more to you than ten sons?" (1:8). He offers himself, the husband on the ground, and the weeping and the not eating stay exactly as they were. The one thing missing is seed, and he is not that. Nothing outside the woman moves the state. It moves when she files.

The Vow — I AM Assumed Inside the Enclosure

And she made an oath, and said, O Lord of armies, if you will truly take note of the sorrow of your servant, not turning away from me but keeping me in mind, and will give me a man-child, then I will give him to the Lord all the days of his life, and his hair will never be cut. — 1 Samuel 1:11

Inside the enclosure, Hannah does not ask to feel better. She files a specific outcome. "Man-child" is zera 'anashim, "seed of men" (H2233, H376), and zera is the word of Genesis 1:11, 1:12 and 1:29. She asks for the seed of Genesis 1:11, and she gives the child to the court before he exists. This is the precise mechanics of Ask, Believe, Receive: YHVH occupying the I AM as already true, filing the identity before the evidence appears. It is spoken from inside the lack, from inside the enclosure, the same structure as Jonah declaring salvation from inside the fish. Elohim receive it, and are now bound to enforce it.

The Face — Identity Shift Before Delivery

"So the woman went away, and took part in the feast, and her face was no longer sad" (1:18). After the claim is filed and ruled on in the name of the judges, and before Hannah has conceived, her face changes and she eats, which is the thing she would not do in 1:7. The identity has shifted inside the enclosure and the outward evidence has not yet followed. This is the court's sequence: the I AM is assumed internally, Elohim register the filing, and the outward result follows according to the statute. Then the order of 1:19: they return to Ramah, to their house, Elkanah "had connection with his wife", and "the Lord kept her in mind". YHVH, present consciousness, has moved to the new state on the same ground.

Samuel — The Court Delivers After Its Kind

Samuel (Strong's H8050) is "heard of God" in Strong's, and BDB's outline reads "his name is El". The verse's own reason for the name is the verb asked: "I made a prayer to the Lord for him" (1:20, BBE) is sha'al, H7592, "I asked him". The same verb is in 1:17, 1:27 and 1:28, where it is the word the KJV prints as "lent". The seed that was asked for is given after its kind and lent back to the one it was asked of. Chapter 2 closes the pair: Hannah is given more sons and daughters (2:21), the same category the other wife held in 1:2. The two named states on one ground both end up holding sons and daughters, and the one that filed the claim is the one that moved. The vocabulary was set on the days of creation. Hannah and Peninnah runs every thread.
