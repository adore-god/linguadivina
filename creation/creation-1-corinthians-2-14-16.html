The Court & The Creation

1 Corinthians 2:14–16 — The Natural Man and the Declared Mind

But the natural man does not receive the things of the Spirit of God, for they are foolishness to him, and he is not able to know them, because they are judged by the Spirit. But he who is spiritual judges all things, but he himself is judged by no one. For who has known the mind of the Lord, that he may instruct him? But we have the mind of Christ. — 1 Corinthians 2:14–16

The seven days of creation are Elohim — the reader's own internal court — forming an identity through a sequence of judgments and placing that identity into the conditions those judgments produced. Every declaration of good across the seven days is the internal court establishing a category of the reader's own formed state. The man does not construct his position. He is placed into what the court already declared. This is what the whole Bible narrative operates from and what it is teaching throughout: the identity is formed by the internal judgments first, and the placement follows. Paul in 1 Corinthians 2:14–16 is addressing the reader's relationship to that same formation. Most readers approach the text as the natural man — YHVH, present consciousness, receiving the court's instruction with no assumed identity in place to receive it. The passage is not describing someone else. It is describing the reader's own position relative to what Elohim established at the beginning. The instrument the passage names is the declared mind — and the court enforces after its kind.

The Natural Man — Genesis 1:2, The Formless Deep

Genesis 1:2 — darkness on the face of the deep, no form, no declaration. The natural man of 1 Corinthians 2:14 occupies this condition. YHVH is present but absorbs only the existing state, presenting no assumed identity to the internal court. Elohim — the judges and rulers — enforces what is presented. When nothing is assumed, the default state is what the court enforces back. The court's instruction reads as foolishness not because it is obscure but because the natural man has no operative I AM from which to receive it. The deep does not reject the light. It simply has not declared it yet.

The Spiritual Man Judges All Things — Genesis 1:3, The Declaration

Genesis 1:3 — the court declares light before light exists externally. This is the statute the spiritual man runs. To judge all things is not to evaluate from a position of superiority. It is the Genesis mechanic of declaration preceding enforcement — YHVH assuming the I AM first, filing the identity with the internal court before external evidence arrives. Elohim receives the declaration and is bound to enforce it. The spiritual man does not wait for the external world to confirm the identity. He declares it. The court moves accordingly.

Judged By No One — The Assumed Identity Held

To be judged by no one is not immunity from accountability. It is the precise opposite of the natural man's condition: YHVH not subjugating the assumed I AM back down to the reactive mind. The declaration already filed with the court is not overwritten by external circumstance. Thread 7 of the framework names the failure mode precisely — sin as the jurisdictional error, the false filing, YHVH presenting a fragmented identity that contradicts the assumed I AM. To be judged by no one is to hold the declaration intact. Elohim enforces what is presented. The spiritual man does not present the contradiction.

The Mind of Christ — The Anointed, Set Apart State

But we have the mind of Christ. — 1 Corinthians 2:16

Christos in Greek means anointed — set apart. The mind of Christ is not a theological elevation reserved for particular figures. It is the fully assumed and held I AM — the identity set apart from the default state, operational within the internal court, and therefore what Elohim is bound to enforce. Theology has made this phrase exclusive and supernatural. The text makes it structural and available. YHVH assumes the anointed state. Elohim — the judges and rulers of that I AM — enforces it. The natural man remains in Genesis 1:2. The declared mind operates from Genesis 1:3. The vocabulary was set on the days of creation. 1 Corinthians 2:14–16 runs every thread.
