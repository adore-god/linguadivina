The Court & The Creation

1 Samuel 1 — Hannah — The Court Hears the Claim Nobody Heard

And with grief in her soul, weeping bitterly, she made her prayer to the Lord. And she made an oath, and said, O Lord of armies, if you will truly take note of the sorrow of your servant, not turning away from me but keeping me in mind, and will give me a man-child, then I will give him to the Lord all the days of his life, and his hair will never be cut. — 1 Samuel 1:10–11

Before Hannah speaks a word, the passage names the ground she stands on, the line her husband comes from, and the two priests already inside the enclosure at Shiloh. These are not background details. Every name is an identity code, and the chapter runs the way the names read. The passage is a demonstration of what the court does with a claim filed without sound: the officer at the door reads only the outside of it and rules wine, Elohim hear it anyway, the face changes before anything has arrived, and the seed comes after its kind. The Genesis creation pattern is already in the ground before the story opens. The instrument the court uses throughout is the claim filed in the heart — the one thing that Elohim hear and that no officer at the door can read.

The Names — Genesis Day Three, The Ground Named Before She Stands on It

Verse 1 gives the ground and the line before the woman appears. Ramathaim-zophim, H7436, is "double height of watchers" in Strong's, in the hill-country of Ephraim, H669, "double fruit". Elkanah, H511, is "God has obtained", built from El and qanah (H7069), son of Jeroham, H3395, compassionate; son of Elihu, H453, "God of him"; son of Tohu, H8459, abasement; son of Zuph, H6689, honeycomb. Read from the bottom up, the line runs honeycomb, abasement, the Judges of him, compassion, and arrives at the one whom Elohim have obtained. Genesis 1:9-12 names the land and puts plants with seed and fruit-trees in it before the man is placed there, and 1 Samuel 1 names its ground the same way: double height, double fruit, sweetness. Nothing about the ground is lacking.

One name needs a check. Tohu in verse 1 is not the tohu of Genesis 1:2. It has its own number, H8459, from an unused root meaning to depress, and it occurs once. The formless word of Genesis 1:2 is H8414, from an unused root meaning to lie waste, and occurs twenty times. The name carries abasement and nothing more, so no link is drawn. A second note: the BBE reads "Ramathaim, a Zuphite" where Strong's reads one place name, and the lexicons disagree. What the text says is missing is not on the ground. It is on the woman: "Hannah had no children" (1:2). The ground is named fruitful, the line is named obtained, and the one missing piece is seed.

Hophni and Phinehas — Genesis Plurality at the Door

Verse 3 names two priests at Shiloh, the two sons of Eli. Hophni, H2652, is "perhaps pugilist" in Strong's, from H2651. Phinehas, H6372, is "mouth of a serpent" in Strong's, and "mouth of brass" in the outline Blue Letter Bible carries. The lexicons differ, so these glosses are readings, and the narrative does the interpreting. Its own verdict comes in chapter 2, which calls them sons of Belial (H1100, 2:12) — Strong's gives "without profit, worthlessness". Elohim — the judges and rulers — are plural from Genesis 1:26, and two named figures at the door of the enclosure are the court's own plurality shown as two states, already inside before Hannah arrives. A filing that produces nothing is what the word Belial names. The court is not ignoring them. It enforces what each presents, impartially, after its kind.

The Closed Womb — Genesis 1:28, The Blessing Held in One State

Hannah, H2584, is "favored" in Strong's, from chanan, H2603. She holds a condition that contradicts her name. The BBE says "the Lord had not let her have children" (1:5, and again in 1:6): the text puts the holding with YHVH, present consciousness, and Elohim hold what is held after its kind, year after year. Peninnah, H6444, is "jewel" in BDB's outline. Strong's gives "probably from H6443", which Strong's glosses "probably a pearl" and the KJV prints "ruby", so the lexicons differ on the stone and this article rests on "jewel". She "was the mother of children" (1:2) and receives portions for "all her sons and daughters" (1:4). Two states stand on one ground: the one named Favored holding no seed, and the one named Jewel holding the sons and daughters of the Genesis 1:28 blessing of fruitfulness.

Year by year, whenever Hannah goes up to the house of the Lord, the other wife "kept on attacking her, so that Hannah gave herself up to weeping and would take no food" (1:7). Elkanah asks: "am I not more to you than ten sons?" (1:8). He offers himself, the husband on the ground, and the weeping and the not eating stay exactly as they were. What is missing is seed, and he is not that. The court has already provided the enclosure. The question is what YHVH will file inside it.

Shiloh — Genesis Day Seven, The Rest the Filing Is Made In

Shiloh, H7887, is "tranquil" in Strong's, from the same as H7886. Genesis 2:2 — the court rested on the seventh day. The yearly going up (1:3, 1:7) takes Hannah into the place named for rest every year, and it is there, after the meal in the guest room, that she gets up (1:9) and files. This framework does not read that as a religious duty. It is the movement of YHVH into the one condition from which a new I AM can be filed without striving. The court always uses its own established enclosures, the way it used the fish for Jonah. Hophni and Phinehas are already inside this one, and Hannah enters the same place. What she files there decides what Elohim are bound to deliver.

The Silent Prayer — The Court Hears What the Door Cannot Read

For Hannah's prayer came from her heart, and though her lips were moving she made no sound: so it seemed to Eli that she was overcome with wine. — 1 Samuel 1:13

Her claim has a precise shape. "Man-child" in the BBE is zera 'anashim, "seed of men" (H2233, H376). Zera is the word of Genesis 1:11, 1:12 and 1:29, and BDB files 1 Samuel 1:11 under "a particular child (son)". She asks for the seed of Genesis 1:11. The filing is complete: what is asked (seed), who must hold it ("keeping me in mind"), and where it goes afterward ("I will give him to the Lord all the days of his life"). It is also silent. Eli, H5941, "lofty" in Strong's, from H5927 to go up, is seated "by the pillars of the doorway of the Temple" (1:9), and verse 12 says he "was watching her mouth". He reads the outside of the claim, lips moving and no sound, and rules wine. The I AM is not declared outward to be verified by outer judges. It is assumed within, and the filing travels where the lofty officer at the door cannot follow.

Eli — Genesis 1:26, The Officer of the Court at the Door

Eli is not opposed to Hannah. He is the structural limit of the high place. He sits at the doorway of the enclosure at the height the institution gives him, and he can rule only on what shows. Hannah answers with where the claim actually is: "I have not taken wine or strong drink, but I have been opening my heart before the Lord" (1:15). Then comes the ruling: "Go in peace: and may the God of Israel give you an answer to the prayer you have made to him" (1:17). The BBE prints "God of Israel" there; the word is Elohim, the plural court. The ruling is given in the name of the judges, to a claim addressed to YHVH. That is YHVH and Elohim in two verses: YHVH is asked, Elohim give. The officer does not generate the verdict. The court uses his position to confirm the enforcement and not to initiate it.

The Word Belial — The Same Word Lands Twice

Do not take your servant to be a good-for-nothing woman: for my words have come from my stored-up sorrow and pain. — 1 Samuel 1:16

"Good-for-nothing" is bat beliyya'al, "daughter of Belial" (H1100), which BDB glosses "drunken woman" at this verse. The same word is used in 2:12, where the sons of Eli are sons of Belial. H1100 occurs twenty-seven times, and 1:16 and 2:12 are both on the list. Hannah is asking not to be ruled on as worthlessness, which is exactly what a filing that produces nothing would be. The jurisdictional error is the filing that misses its mark, and she asks to be read by her filing and not by her lips. Hannah is not drawing a comparison with the priests. The narrative sets the same word beside them, and the reader sees it land twice: once on the woman asking for seed, and once on the two at the door.

The Face Changed — Genesis Day Three, Seed Before the Evidence

"So the woman went away, and took part in the feast, and her face was no longer sad" (1:18). The not-eating of 1:7 has lifted. Samuel has not been conceived and the womb is still as it was, but the I AM has moved: YHVH is no longer occupying the state of lack, because the claim is filed and has been ruled on in the name of the judges. Ask, Believe, Receive: the believing is the changed face, and not the arrival of the child. Then the order of the verse: "they got up, and after worshipping before the Lord they went back to Ramah, to their house: and Elkanah had connection with his wife; and the Lord kept her in mind" (1:19). Back on the same ground, union, then the verdict. Ramah, H7414, is the height, and Ramathaim is the same place. No old state is left in the chapter. The ground is kept and what is held on it changes. Genesis 4:1 is built the same way: union, conception, birth, and then the child's name is explained.

Samuel — After Its Kind, The Asking Named

Now the time came when Hannah, being with child, gave birth to a son; and she gave him the name Samuel, Because, she said, I made a prayer to the Lord for him. — 1 Samuel 1:20

Samuel, H8050, is "heard of God" in Strong's, from the passive participle of shama, H8085, and El, H410. BDB's outline reads "his name is El" and notes that 1:20 explains the name as if from sha'al. The verse's own reason is that verb: "I asked him", sha'al, H7592. The same verb runs through the chapter. It is in Eli's ruling in 1:17 ("you have asked"), in 1:20, in Hannah's report in 1:27, and in 1:28, where it is the word the KJV prints as "lent". The BBE uses other English words each time, so the repeat does not show. The seed was asked for, it was given after its kind, and it is lent back to the one it was asked of. The name records what happened in the court: a claim nobody heard, heard.

The two at the door are enforced the same way. Chapter 2 gives them a sign that death will overtake both on the same day (2:34). What each presents is what the court delivers, impartially. The same mechanism that moved through Abraham, Isaac, Jacob, and Joseph moves through Hannah: the identity filed before the evidence, Elohim enforcing what the name already carries. The vocabulary was set on the days of creation. Hannah runs every thread.
