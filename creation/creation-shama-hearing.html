The Court & The Creation

Shama — The Court's Reception Mechanism

Hear, O Israel: the Lord our God is one Lord. — Deuteronomy 6:4 (BBE)

The word translated hear in the Shema is shama — H8085 in Strong's, a primitive Hebrew root meaning to hear intelligently, to consent, to receive a verdict, to align with what is declared. The judicial sense is built into the root: shama contains both the reception and the compliance in a single term. This is the operative word for how the court receives an identity filing and how YHVH, present consciousness, receives the court's declarations in return. The same root names Simeon, Ishmael, Samuel, Elishama, and Simon — not as shared etymology but as the same function tracked across the entire biblical narrative in five distinct modes. The court heard. The court will hear. The function named before the life carries it. The function held as a standing office. The capacity present in a figure, and that same capacity when it is operating. Each name is an identity code for a different stage of shama. The court's instrument for reception is shama. The vocabulary was fixed at creation. Shama runs every thread.

The Root — Shama H8085

Strong's gives shama's full range as: to hear, to hear intelligently, to give attention, to consent, to grant a request, to hear judicially, to obey. Every sense in that range points toward the same mechanism. When Elohim shamas an identity filing, the court is not merely detecting it — it is receiving it as a valid claim, ratifying it, and committing to enforce it. When YHVH shamas the court's declaration, present consciousness is not merely noting it — it is receiving the verdict, aligning with it, and occupying the identity that has been declared. Shama without the alignment is not yet shama in its full sense. After its kind: a figure named from this root carries the function as an encoded identity. The narrative unfolds by demonstrating Elohim enforcing what the name already declares — including when the function is present but not yet active, and when it drops out entirely.

Simeon — The name as the Court's Receipt

Then she became with child again, and gave birth to a son; and said, Because it has come to the Lord's ears that I am not loved, he has given me this son in addition: and she gave him the name Simeon. — Genesis 29:33 (BBE)

Leah names her second son Simeon — H8095, from shama. It has come to the Lord's ears that I am not loved — the unfavoured state, the prior condition, the formlessness before the declaration opens a new state. Simeon is born because the court registered what Leah's condition was filing with it. The name she gives him is the record of that reception: past tense, already accomplished. The court heard. This is the first instance in the narrative of shama producing a son from a state of lack, and it sets the pattern the thread carries. The filing came from affliction. The court received it. The name encodes the reception as a permanent record. Elohim enforces after its kind — the child named for the court's hearing carries that function into the line of Israel.

Ishmael — The Name as the Court's Forward Declaration

See, you are with child and will give birth to a son, to whom you will give the name Ishmael, because the ears of the Lord were open to your sorrow. — Genesis 16:11 (BBE)

Hagar flees into the wilderness — outside the enclosure, outside the household, in the prior state. The court names the child before he exists: Ishmael — H3458, from shama and El, El will hear or God hears. Where Simeon's name is a receipt — the court heard, past tense, from the state of lack — Ishmael's name is a forward declaration. The ears of the court were open to the sorrow before the resolution came. The name is declared over Hagar in the wilderness before the child is born, before the affliction is lifted, before anything in the situation has changed. The I AM is filed before the evidence appears. Abraham's line carries both tenses of the shama function: Simeon records the court heard; Ishmael declares the court will hear. Elohim enforces the name. The narrative of Ishmael's life unfolds from what his name already encodes — the one the court has declared it will hear.

Samuel — The Function Named Before the Life Carries It

Hannah's vow in Shiloh produces a child she names Samuel — H8050, heard of Elohim. Her lips move without sound inside the court's enclosure. Eli, at the doorpost, cannot receive the filing — it does not travel through any outer channel. The court receives it directly. The name Hannah gives the child before he is born is not a description of the birth. It is a declaration of the function he will carry: heard of Elohim is what Samuel will be throughout his life — the one through whom the court's verdicts reach Israel when they reach no one else. The name is the operating instruction placed before the life begins. Elohim enforces after its kind: the child named for the court's reception function becomes the figure who carries that function across an entire era.

Samuel Chapter Three — The Name Present Before the Function Activates

And the Lord came and took his place there, crying as at other times, Samuel, Samuel. Then Samuel said, Say on; for your servant is listening. — 1 Samuel 3:10 (BBE)

Samuel carries the name heard of Elohim from birth. In chapter three he has not yet entered the function the name encodes. YHVH calls his name three times in the night. Each time Samuel runs to Eli. The name is present; the function is not running. This is the same distinction the text tracks later in Simon and Peter — and it appears here first, in Samuel, with unmistakable clarity. Eli identifies the pattern and gives Samuel the formula: if the voice comes again, say speak, for your servant hears. The moment Samuel says it, the shama function activates. From that point forward he operates as what his name declared. The name contained the function before the life entered it. The identity was established before the history demonstrated it.

Elishama — The Function as a Standing Office

And they went in to the king in the open square, putting the roll in the room of Elishama the secretary; and they gave the king an account of all these words. — Jeremiah 36:20 (BBE)

Elishama — H476, from El and shama: God of hearing, or my El hears. Where Simeon records the reception, Ishmael receives the forward declaration, and Samuel embodies the function as a calling, Elishama holds it as a standing office. He is the scribe. The scroll — Jeremiah's dictated word of the court — is kept in his chamber. The court's word sits in the room of the one whose name means the court's hearing. When the king sends for it, it is retrieved from Elishama's chamber. The function is not a momentary event here. It is an institution: the court's word held in permanent custody by the one named for its reception. After its kind: the name governs the role. Elohim enforces what the identity already declares.

Simon — The Function Named; Peter — The Function Operating

And Jesus said to him, A blessing on you, Simon Bar-jonah: because this was not made clear to you by flesh and blood, but by my Father in heaven. And I say to you that you are Peter, and on this rock my church will be based. — Matthew 16:17–18 (BBE)

Simon — G4613 in Strong's, from the Hebrew shimeon, a hearing. The same root carried into the New Testament as an identity code. Simon's function is to receive the court's verdicts. At Caesarea Philippi that function operates: YHVH receives the identity of the I AM and declares it when no outer transmission has carried it. The shama function runs correctly — and the name shifts in the same declaration. A blessing on you, Simon Bar-jonah — the function receiving. You are Peter — what the function produces when it operates: the rock, the foundation, the structure Elohim builds on. Simon is the function. Peter is the function operating. The two names track two states of the same capacity in real time within a single declaration.

The Name Tracks the State — Matthew 16:23, Luke 22, Mark 14

Simon, Simon, see, Satan has been desiring to have you, to put you through his test as grain is put through a process to get out the grain. — Luke 22:31 (BBE)

Within a single exchange in Luke 22:31–34, the text says Simon, Simon — then three verses later addresses him as Peter. The function is about to fail: Simon. The declaration is delivered to the function even in its impending failure: Peter, the cock will not cry before you say three times that you have no knowledge of me. Two names, one exchange, two states of the same capacity marked in sequence. In Matthew 16:23, moments after Caesarea Philippi, the same figure blocks the court's declared path: Get behind me, Satan. The capacity named to receive and transmit the court's verdicts is actively refusing one. The rock becomes the stumbling block — the sharpest form of the failure, and it receives the sharpest address from the court. In Mark 14:37, in Gethsemane: Simon, are you sleeping? The function has dropped out at the moment it is most needed. The name used is Simon. The pattern is consistent from Simeon onward: the text does not vary the name arbitrarily. It uses the name to record the state of the function.

John 21 — The Function Recalled Through Three Questions

He said to him the third time, Simon, son of John, is your heart with me? Peter was troubled because he said to him the third time, Is your heart with me? And he said, Lord, you have knowledge of all things; you see that I have love for you. — John 21:17 (BBE)

Three denials produced the failure. Three questions address the restoration — every one addressed to Simon son of John. Not Peter. Simon. The function is being recalled to itself by its own name before it can operate again. The re-filing runs through the name. Three times the capacity is addressed at its root. Three times it is asked whether it is oriented toward the I AM — is your heart with me, do you align with what is declared. The third time Simon is troubled: the record that the recall has reached the function at depth. From Pentecost onward the figure operates as Peter. John 21 is the last moment in the entire narrative that the name Simon is used. The restoration closes the line exactly where it opened: the function recalled by its name, the name restored to its function.

The Shema — The Court Calling YHVH to Activate the Function

Deuteronomy 6:4 — Shema Yisrael, YHVH Elohenu, YHVH Echad. Hear, O Israel: YHVH our Elohim, YHVH is one. The operative verb is shama. The foundational declaration of the framework is the court calling YHVH to activate the reception function — to hear intelligently, to receive the declared identity, to align with it and occupy it. Elohim — the judges and rulers — declares the unified I AM: YHVH is one. YHVH, present consciousness, is called to shama that declaration and hold it. The five names in this thread are five modes of that same call: Simeon — the court received the filing from the state of lack; Ishmael — the court declares it will hear before anything changes; Samuel — the function named before the life demonstrates it; Elishama — the office that holds the court's word in permanent custody; Simon — the capacity present in consciousness; Peter — the same capacity when it is running. The Shema is the mechanism in its most compressed form: the court calling its own reception function into operation. The vocabulary was set on the days of creation. Shama runs every thread.
