The Court & The Creation

Genesis 14 — Melchizedek and Abraham: The Court Ratifies the Assumed Identity

And Melchizedek, king of Salem, the priest of the Most High God, took bread and wine, And blessing him, said, May the blessing of the Most High God, maker of heaven and earth, be on Abram: And let the Most High God be praised, who has given into your hands those who were against you. Then Abram gave him a tenth of all the goods he had taken. — Genesis 14:18–20

Abram comes back with the people and goods he has won back, and two kings meet him in the same valley. The king of Sodom offers goods. The king of Salem takes bread and wine, speaks a blessing, and receives a tenth. Genesis 14:18–20 is not hospitality and it is not a reward for winning a fight. It shows what the court does when two offers stand on the same ground in front of one identity: the blessing is received, the tenth goes back to its source, and nothing is taken that would credit the increase to another. Every element maps to a category the court fixed at the beginning of creation — the ground, the leaving, the name, the plants, the blessing, the seed. The court's instrument is a priest whose name holds the verdict before the meeting begins and whose text lists no father.

Lot in Sodom — The Covering on Burning Ground, Genesis Day Three

The text places Lot, Abram's brother's son, as one "living in Sodom" (14:12). Lot (H3876) means covering, the same family as the word for a veil (H3875). Sodom (H5467) is a burnt district, from a root meaning to scorch. Before the meeting, the four kings take "all the goods and food from Sodom and Gomorrah" (14:11) and then Lot and all his goods (14:12), and the ground itself is "full of holes of sticky earth" (14:10). On day three the court speaks the ground into bringing up grass, seed-plant and fruit-tree, each after its kind (Genesis 1:11–12). A covering lodged on burning ground is enforced as burning-ground goods: Elohim enforces after its kind, and what lives in that place is carried off with the place when others rule it. The name already told the story. The covering sat in the burning.

Abram Pursues to Dan — Leave and Cleave, a House Built After Leaving

Abram (H87) means high father. He takes "trained men, three hundred and eighteen of them, sons of his house" and goes after the captors "as far as Dan" (14:14), then "got back all the goods, and Lot" (14:16). Genesis 12 sent him out of his father's house; the house he commands here is one he built after leaving. That is leave and cleave carrying weight: the old familiar state is left, and what is built after it has men of its own. Dan (H1835) means judge, from H1777, and this is where the pursuit reaches. The covering is won back by going to the judge. Elohim is the judges and rulers, and the name of the place says which bench the identity went to.

Melchizedek — The First Priest, Genesis 1:26 and the Name Before the Narrative

The Hebrew word for priest (kohen, H3548) is first used in 14:18. No priest stands in the text before this one, and none is given a father, mother or family. The name carries what lineage would otherwise carry: Melchizedek is king (H4428) of righteousness (tsedeq, H6664), and Salem (H8004) is peaceful. This is names as identity codes: the name discloses the nature of the state before the narrative opens. Genesis 1:26 makes identity the creative unit, and here an identity is met whose nature is already stated in its name, righteousness and peace together. Melchizedek is not the name for a state Abram has reached. Abram is still High-father, and he receives the blessing, which is how the two are told apart. YHVH as present awareness meets the identity that is already right and at peace, and it does not become that identity by being blessed by it.

Bread and Wine — Genesis Day Three Without the Sweat

The BBE says Melchizedek "took bread and wine." Day three is where the court speaks the seed-plants and fruit-trees, and bread and wine come from grain and vine. The word for bread (H3899) is first used in Genesis 3:19, as what the man will eat from the cursed ground by the sweat of his face. This is its second use, and it is handed to Abram. The word for wine (H3196) is first used in Noah's tent (9:21, 9:24), where it ends in nakedness and a curse; its next use is here. Nothing in this scene is worked for or grabbed. The ground's fruit arrives with a blessing. The botanical vocabulary of Genesis has not changed. What has changed is who is holding it out.

El Elyon — The Governing Structure at Full Jurisdiction

Melchizedek is "the priest of the Most High God," and the Hebrew is El Elyon. El (H410) is the root of Elohim, the judges and rulers of I AM, the plural structure that enforces whatever identity is dominantly assumed. Elyon (H5945) is Strong's "an elevation, i.e. lofty; as title, the Supreme," from H5927, to go up, and it is first used in this scene. The blessing names this bench as "maker of heaven and earth." The Hebrew behind "maker" is qoneh (from H7069), Strong's "to erect, i.e. create; to procure; to own," so the bench is named as the possessor of the whole scene. El Elyon is Elohim named at full height, and the I AM it enforces is the one held when the covering was won back from burning ground.

The Blessing — Genesis Day Five and Six, Be Fruitful

In Genesis the court's first blessings are spoken over what it has made: the sea creatures and birds, then the man, with the word to be fruitful (1:22, 1:28). The blessing is the court's own word, and what follows it after its kind is increase. In Genesis 12 the Existing One blesses Abram. Here the priest speaks the blessing over him, "May the blessing of the Most High God ... be on Abram," and then blesses the Most High for what has been given into his hands. The one who blesses is the source the identity is issued from. This is the same mechanism as the court's first blessings, running through a person, and the proof of a blessing is what comes after it. The next chapter gives it: seed.

The Tenth — Ask, Believe, Receive: Given Before the Seed Arrives

"Then Abram gave him a tenth of all the goods he had taken." The word for tenth (H4643) is first used here, and the priest who receives it is the first priest in the text. What Abram won back is not handed to the king of Sodom and is not kept. It is returned to where the blessing came from. It is not payment. It is the identity acknowledging its source, and it happens before the evidence arrives: in 15:2–3 Abram still says "I have no child." That is the sequence of Ask, Believe, Receive. The acknowledgment is made first, and the seed follows.

The Offer of Burning — The Filing Credited to Another Source, Genesis 1:1

But Abram said to the king of Sodom, I have taken an oath to the Lord, the Most High God, maker of heaven and earth, That I will not take so much as a thread or the cord of a shoe of yours; so that you may not say, I have given wealth to Abram: — Genesis 14:22–23

The king of Sodom speaks only after Melchizedek has acted: "Give me the prisoners and take the goods for yourself" (14:21). In the Hebrew of 14:22, Abram puts YHVH, El Elyon and the possessor of heaven and earth into one sentence. Present awareness and the court are addressed together, and his hand is lifted on it. Because the possessor owns the scene, Burning has nothing that is its to give, and a filing that read "I have given wealth to Abram" would credit Abram's increase to the burning ground. The court enforces the filing presented. So he takes nothing of Burning's, only the food the fighting men have eaten, and his allies Aner, Eshcol and Mamre have their share (14:24).

Abram at the Threshold — Day Six, the Name Before the Name

After these things, the word of the Lord came to Abram in a vision, saying, Have no fear, Abram: I will keep you safe, and great will be your reward. ... And he took him out into the open air, and said to him, Let your eyes be lifted to heaven, and see if the stars may be numbered; even so will your seed be. And he had faith in the Lord, and it was put to his account as righteousness. — Genesis 15:1, 5–6

At this moment the man is still Abram, High-father, not yet Abraham, father of many. The fuller name arrives in Genesis 17. The reward Burning could not give is now spoken: seed like the stars. Then comes the word for righteousness (tsedaqah, H6666), used for the first time in the Bible, built on the same root as the second half of Melchizedek's name (H6663). King-of-righteousness blesses, and righteousness is put to Abram's account. Day six is the category of man given increase, and the court does not skip a category. The seed contains the harvest before the harvest is visible.

The Valley — The King's Valley, One Level Ground

The two kings meet Abram "in the valley of Shaveh, that is, the King's Valley" (14:17). Shaveh (H7740) means plain, level ground. Both offers are made in the open, on the same ground, one after the other. Burning says take the goods. The king of Peace brings bread, wine and a blessing. The identity takes the blessing, gives a tenth to the first priest, refuses a thread of Burning, and has righteousness put to its account in the next chapter. The creation story did not fix its categories as background. It fixed them as the working vocabulary of the court, the instruments through which every identity shift and every enforcement is conducted. The vocabulary was set on the days of creation. Melchizedek and Abraham run every thread.
