About Lingua Divina

What This Site Is

The Bible is written with a fixed vocabulary. The words that appear in the opening chapter of Genesis — light, seed, vine, shepherd, breath, deep, dry land — do not change across the text. They reappear in the same way, carrying the same meaning, from the first page to the last. This site traces that vocabulary.

Once you see it, the Bible reads differently. Isaiah's stump, the mustard seed, the lost sheep, the vine and branches, the heart of the earth — these are not independent images. They are the creation story's own words being used again. The whole of Scripture is written in the language Genesis established. Every narrative draws from the same source.

From that starting point, the three names the Bible uses for God also come into focus. Elohim — the first word in Genesis 1:1 — is plural. It means judges and rulers. YHVH is the existing one, the present state of the person in the narrative. Ehyeh — I AM — is what Exodus 3:14 names as the operative declaration: I AM that I AM. When those three terms are read as they actually appear in the Hebrew, the structure of every story becomes legible. Characters, names, and events follow from that structure consistently.

No doctrine is imposed. No denomination is represented. The interpretations stay inside the text.
