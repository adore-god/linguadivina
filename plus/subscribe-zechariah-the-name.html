The Court & The Creation

Zechariah — The Root the Court Speaks, Loses, and Recovers

And God kept Noah in mind, and all the living things and the cattle which were with him in the ark: and God sent a wind over the earth, and the waters went down. — Genesis 8:1

Zechariah — Jah has remembered, from the Hebrew זְכַרְיָה, built from zakar, "to remember," and Jah — is the name of twenty-nine Israelites. But the root inside the name is older than any of them. Genesis 8:1 uses zakar for the first time in the whole Hebrew Bible, and it is not yet attached to anyone's name — it is the court's own action, ending an enclosure by remembering what is sealed inside it. Every man who later carries this name inherits a state the root already defined before he existed.

This is a name study, not a passage study: it follows one root — zakar — through every position it occupies across scripture, in the order the mechanism runs. The root appears as pure origin, arriving before anything is asked. It is invoked as an appeal, called on after the court that should carry it forgets to. It is withheld until a man actually occupies it. And it returns as a promise, no longer describing what the court does for one man but what the once-scattered will do for the court in return. Four positions, one root. What is constant is Elohim — the judges and rulers — enforcing the same root after its kind, regardless of which enclosure carries it.

The Ark and the Deep — Genesis 8:1, the Root Before the Name

Before the root occupies any of its four positions, it appears once with no man attached to it at all. The Hebrew verb zakar occurs for the first time in the entire Hebrew Bible at Genesis 8:1: the court keeps Noah in mind, and the waters begin to go down. The deep — Genesis 1:2's formless waters, the same category Jonah is returned to and the same category the Spirit moves over before a single day is spoken — is here running in reverse. Where day one opens with darkness on the face of the deep, Genesis 8:1 closes an extended version of that same darkness: the flood, the ark as enclosure, and the court's own remembering as the single act that ends the containment. The root is not primarily about memory in the sentimental sense. It is about the court terminating a period of enclosure by turning its attention back to what was sealed inside it. This is the zero-point. Every later occurrence of the name Zechariah inherits it.

Zechariah the Prophet — the Root as Origin

The first position: the root arriving before anything is asked.
