Nahum — The Court Refuses to Sow the Name Again

The word about Nineveh. The book of the vision of Nahum the Elkoshite. — Nahum 1:1

Nahum reopens a file, not a new indictment, but the file itself goes back further than Jonah. Nineveh's identity does not begin in either book. It begins in Genesis 10:8-11, where Nimrod's kingdom starts at Babel and extends outward to build Nineveh — the same builder, the same project. Genesis 11:4 records what that project was: "let us make us a name (shem, H8034)... lest we be scattered abroad." A name assumed by the builders themselves, not declared by the court. That is the identity Nineveh carries into every later book. Jonah re-planted it and gave it continuance. That continuance has run out, and the record is now examined again, all the way back to the name it was founded on. Nahum himself is named "the Elkoshite" (H512) — the lexicons carry this as "God the ensnarer," even while the place behind it has never been identified. The location is unfiled; the name is not. His own title already names the mechanism he carries: Nineveh will be charged in 3:4 with taking nations in a net through deceit, and the one bringing that charge is himself titled for a snare. His own name is Nahum (H5151), comfort, from nacham. Like any name in this record, it is brought to fruition, not left as decoration — but it comes to fruition for the party he speaks for, not for the one he speaks against. Judah receives it: the yoke broken and chains parted in 1:12-13, the good news and peace of 1:15, the vine of Jacob made good in 2:2. Nineveh runs the opposite ruling in the same book, at the same time. One file, two names, two verdicts, each brought to fruition after its own kind.
