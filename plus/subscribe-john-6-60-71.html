John 6:60-71 — The Hard Saying — The Court Divides What It Has Spoken

Then, hearing this, a number of his disciples said, This is a hard saying; who is able to take in such teaching? — John 6:60

This passage closes the discourse spoken in the synagogue at Capernaum — Kaphar Nachum, "the village of comfort." Everything before this point has built toward one claim: that present consciousness must occupy the identity at the source level, not at the level of bread that perishes and leaves the fathers dead. Now the crowd hits the limit of what it can assume. This is not a passage about people abandoning a teacher. It is a demonstration of the court sorting present consciousness by what it can occupy at the level of origin rather than the level of form. Elohim — the judges and rulers — enforces after its kind, and the court's instrument throughout this passage is the word itself: not manna, not flesh, but the word declared as spirit and as life.

The Hard Saying — Day One, Spirit Upon the Waters

The spirit is the life giver; the flesh is of no value: the words which I have said to you are spirit and they are life. — John 6:63

The crowd measures the discourse against the only category it has — formed bread, formed flesh — and calls it too hard to take in. Jesus answers by naming the actual instrument: spirit, not flesh. This is Genesis 1:2 — the animating principle moving over the face of the waters before any form exists, before dry land, before vegetation, before any created category has been spoken.
