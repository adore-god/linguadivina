The Court & The Creation

The Lamp — Oil Kept Before Light Rules

And God said, Let there be light: and it was so. And God, looking on the light, saw that it was good: and he made a division between the light and the dark. — Genesis 1:3–4

Scripture keeps returning to the same small object: a lamp, a measure of oil, a wick that is either kept burning or let go out. The ten virgins and their vessels, John described as a burning and shining lamp, a lamp set on a stand instead of hidden under a bushel — these are not three separate lessons about watchfulness and testimony. They are one mechanism named three times. I AM, once assumed, has to be supplied, carried, and exercised in the open, or the court has nothing to enforce when the moment of inspection arrives. The instrument running through every occurrence is the lamp itself — the vessel that carries and displays whatever identity YHVH has assumed, under the Genesis creation pattern that fixed light as a category before any vessel existed to hold it.

The Light — Genesis Day One

Genesis 1:3 — light is spoken before there is sun, lamp, or eye to receive it. This is the first verdict of the court, issued into formless darkness, dividing light from dark before any created thing carries it.
