The Court & The Creation

1 Timothy 3:1-7 — The House Proves the Dominion First

This is a true saying, A man desiring the position of a Bishop has a desire for a good work. — 1 Timothy 3:1

Paul is not writing a character reference. He is stating the court's specification for who may occupy an office called episkopos — overseer, from a root meaning one stationed over a mark to watch it. The man who desires this position is declaring an I AM: the dominion category fixed on the sixth day, when the court first assigned present consciousness rule over what it had made. But desiring the office and holding it are not the same filing. Before Elohim — the judges and rulers of I AM — extends dominion over the wider church, the court tests the identical category at the smallest possible scale: one house, one marriage, one shoot in the ground. The instrument the court uses throughout this passage is the household.

The Desire — Genesis 1:26 Dominion

"Let us make man in our image, after our likeness: and let them have dominion." Genesis 1:26 sets the category the overseer now claims. The desire itself is not the fault line; it is the correct first movement — YHVH, present consciousness, presenting an assumed identity for the court to examine.
